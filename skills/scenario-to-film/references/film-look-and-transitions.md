# Film look, composition and scene transitions

Use this when writing reference-image prompts (step 3), the shot list (step 2) and the previs
(step 4). Every cut gets a framing intent, a color role and a way in and out.

## 1. Composition (what one reference music video does well)

Studied from a reference clip supplied by the author (it is not published, so this is the author's observation, not something you can check), sampled once per second (21 frames). Only the
pictures were studied, not the sound or the exact camera paths.

- **Scale contrast.** Extreme close-ups (eyes, lips, hand on feathers) alternate with wide frames
  where the figure is small and centered (crosswalk, skyline silhouette). The jump in scale is
  what makes the cut feel like film.
- **Insert shots.** Small objects and textures carry feeling between faces: a backlit feather in
  a hand, a train window with a sunset, red light rippling in a puddle, moving shadow on a wall.
  Plan 2-3 inserts per minute, each tied to the story's key prop.
- **Depth and layers.** Hair or a foreground blur across the lens, shallow depth of field, a
  frame inside the frame (train window), backlight with soft halation and visible film grain.
- **Center for the hero, edges for context.** In wides the subject sits on the vertical axis with
  leading lines (road, crossing stripes, light streaks) pointing at it.
- **One repeated motif** (a prop or a color) returns at the start, middle and end.

For a story film: begin on the most beautiful frame, keep faces ordinary and specific, and use
scale contrast to mark turns (wide for isolation, close for decision).

## 2. Color script (decide before generating stills)

- Pick two temperatures and one accent per film. Reference: warm amber (comfort, interior,
  golden hour) against cold blue-teal (night city, distance) with red (neon, brake lights, a
  puddle) as the emotional spike. A beige or cream base reads flat; avoid it unless the story asks for it (the author's preference).
- Write the color role per cut in the shot list ("cold, one red accent", "warm, dawn").
- Keep skin natural. Grade the whole film the same way; do not restyle per shot.
- Dawn arc: blue-black to teal to peach is a cheap, readable "relief" for endings.

## 3. Reference-image prompt anatomy (film still / ad still)

Subject and one specific detail; lens and distance (35 mm, 85 mm, macro); light source and
direction (practical, window, neon, backlight); color role; texture (grain, halation, wet
surfaces); composition (centered wide, extreme close-up, over-the-shoulder); one constraint
(no text, natural skin, two hands, correct fingers). One idea per still.

## 4. Scene transitions

Transitions are deliberate, not decorative. Smooth cuts around, one dynamic transition per
sequence. Choose from this list and write it in the shot list.

| Transition | What it is | Use it for | Rule |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hard cut | direct switch | default | motivate every cut with action or look |
| Match cut | similar shape, color, movement or position in both shots | link two ideas or time jumps | match composition and screen position |
| Graphic match | same shape in both frames (eye with light, ripple on puddle) | poetic link | keep the shape in the same part of frame |
| Match on action | action continues across the cut | continuity | end and start in mid movement |
| Contrast cut | quiet close-up to loud wide, warm to cold | emotional jolt | pair with a sound change |
| Smash cut | abrupt, no easing | shock or a joke | needs a large gap in image or sound; use rarely |
| Whip pan cut | fast pan hides the cut in blur | change of place or time, energy | same direction and speed out and in; match brightness |
| Light-trail / speed bridge | streaks of light carry us between places | travel, time passing | short, one per film |
| Black frame | a beat of black | chapter end, breath before the turn | 3-6 frames or up to 1 s |
| Insert / cutaway | a prop shot between two faces | feeling without dialogue | prop must pay off later |
| J cut / L cut | sound arrives before or stays after the picture | smooth emotional bridges | plan the sound lane in the shot list |
| Sound bridge | one sound continues across the cut | link scenes without picture tricks | check it does not leak the next secret |
| Dissolve / wash | slow overlap or color wash | memory, dawn | short, only for a mood change |

Whip pans, cut on action and match cuts only work if both shots are planned together: write the
exit pose and the entry pose in adjacent rows.

## 5. How to use it in the shot list

Add to every row (in notes, not in the table columns the lint script reads): framing intent
(scale: extreme close, close, medium, wide), color role, transition in, transition out. Mark
which cuts carry an insert. Keep at most one whip pan or smash cut per minute.
