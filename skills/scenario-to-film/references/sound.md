# Sound

Write the sound the story needs to hear, take away or leave empty. Sound should change the
viewer's attention, the sense of space or the pressure on a character; listing ambient noise for
every shot without a story job is a failure.

## Five questions per scene

1. What does the viewer hear first, and how does it build the space or an off-screen presence?
2. Which sound stands for the current order, a habit or power?
3. Before which line, proof or choice must the sound be deliberately emptied, and for whom?
   Write the object and the condition that restores it. Empty is not "forgot the track".
   A story without spoken lines empties the sound before the proof, the action or the face
   (for example: the room tone drops just before a cup is handed over).
4. When does ambient sound leave, return or change distance, and what visible event triggers it?
5. Should a sound bridge carry the result into the next scene without revealing too much?

## Rules

- Sound does not replace acting. A reveal does not default to an impact hit; let cutlery stop and
  leave room tone and one swallow.
- Music does not replace a scene turn. If removing the music note makes the choice fail, fix the
  scene first.
- Failure checks: every shot lists ambient sound but the scene has no start, exit, return or turn;
  one impact sound lifts every conflict; silence has no object; a bridge leaks the next scene's
  secret; the plan needs a sound source that exists nowhere in the script.
- A video model's own audio may be good enough. Listen to it before adding layers.
- Per shot, as a default: room tone, one hero effect, one detail effect. Fewer is fine when the
  story needs emptiness; write why in the plan. Music about 12 dB under dialogue.
- A sound source that exists nowhere in the script (a lingering note, an off-screen voice)
  needs a source: add it to a shot or a prop, or drop the sound.

## Where effects come from

Use, in this order: the video model's own audio if it is good, effects the user's approved audio
tool generates, a library whose license allows the use, or a recording. Write the source and the
license (or "generated by <tool>") for every effect in the QA table. Do not take effects from
sites whose license you have not read.

## Mixing is a real step

Writing a sound plan or finishing a render is not a mix. Mixing means ducking, fades, music in and
out and loudness, done with ffmpeg or an editor, with the command or project and the actual
output recorded. After joining shots, listen to every seam for a sudden change in room tone.
Check the end of every voice line for a cut-off syllable. Default target, unless the user or the
platform sets another: integrated loudness about -14 LUFS, true peak at or below -1.5 dBTP, music
bed clearly under the voice (duck about 10-12 dB while speech plays). Measure the final encoded
file (commands in [qa-and-evidence](qa-and-evidence.md)).
