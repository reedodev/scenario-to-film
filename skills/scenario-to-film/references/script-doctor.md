# Script doctor

The story decides everything. Fix it before any image or credit. Write the answers into
`script-review.md`, the rewrite into `script-v2.md`.

## 1. Want and obstacle

One character, one want, one obstacle. If either is missing, add it.

## 2. Four different actions

Do not call one thing by another name:

- **Reveal:** the viewer or character learns an existing fact.
- **Turn:** an existing fact makes a plan, an explanation or a relationship fail.
- **Emotional payoff:** the promised fear, warmth or pain lands as a visible result.
- **Hook:** a new decision or danger keeps the state moving.

Showing a secret that nobody acts on is a reveal, not a turn. Cutting off a last line is
withholding, not a hook.

## 3. Paradox

Find the belief the viewer holds in the first third and the fact that flips it at the end. Write
three candidate paradoxes, pick one, say why. The ending must change what the opening image
means. A weak turn is a stranger announcing that it was all a dream: it changes information but
grows from nothing the character did.

Complete this sentence for the chosen turn:

> Because <established fact or choice>, the original plan <why it no longer holds>, so the
> character can now only act between <cost A> and <cost B>.

## 4. Fair reveal check

Before the turn, answer five questions:

1. Did the viewer see enough signs to explain it afterwards?
2. Who could reach the fact before, and who could not?
3. Why does the proof appear now, not just when the author needs it?
4. Which old explanation does the new fact remove?
5. Who must change their action after learning it?

A prop that sets up the turn must appear before it (for example a gift box on the table before the
retirement is revealed). If a setup was never shown, the reveal is unfair: add the setup shot.

## 5. What the viewer may know now

For every fact whose timing matters, add one line under its shot: source, shown or withheld in
this shot, the visible or audible carrier, release moment, how it is protected (framing, depth,
off-screen sound, light), and why this timing helps.

## 6. Emotion, lightness, cuts

- Name the one shot where the viewer feels it, a small action, not a speech. Everything before
  prepares it, everything after is shorter.
- Put one light or funny beat in the first half so the turn lands harder.
- Give a payoff before the next pressure; "about to find out" is not a payoff.
- Delete any shot that does not feed the paradox, the emotion or the light beat. Keep the length
  inside the contest or platform limit.

## 7. Coverage choice (key scenes only)

For the reveal, the turn and the ending, write two different directing options that change the
viewer's knowledge timing, whose side they stand on or who owns the performance space (not just
shot sizes). Show the trade-offs and let the user choose. Ordinary scenes get no options.

## 8. Honesty

No invented facts, real brands, real people or fake testimonials. Ads show only what the product
really does.

Output a three-line note: the paradox, the shot that carries the emotion, what was cut. The
review is a proposal; the user can overrule it.
